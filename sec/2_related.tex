\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Generated referring language for 3D scenes.}
Most machine-generated 3D referring text comes from one recipe: a scene graph or a program holds the geometry, a template or a constraint sampler turns it into a specification that rules out every distractor, and a language model paraphrases the result~\cite{referit3d,sceneverse,3dgrand,disc3d,clevr3dderef,clevrrefplus}; ViGiL3D++~\cite{vigil3d,vigil3dpp}, the closest work to ours, samples its constraints from real scans. These pipelines agree on three things. Uniqueness is guaranteed on the graph or the program, not on the final sentence: after the paraphrase nothing is checked against the geometry again. The language model changes the words, not what is said; and these pipelines are weakest exactly where human sentences are weakest: on viewpoints, rows and stacks. Most importantly, nobody scores the finished sentence. ViGiL3D++ shows the cost: in its own human check, fewer than half of the sentences whose target has look-alikes were valid~\cite{vigil3dpp}. We differ on all three counts. Verification comes \emph{after} the text, on the final sentence; the model decides what is said, not only how; and every sentence receives a score, even one we did not write, which is how we can measure ScanRefer itself.

\paragraph{The corpora.}
ScanRefer~\cite{scanrefer} attaches one description to one object; Nr3D~\cite{referit3d} does the same through a reference game; Multi3DRefer~\cite{multi3drefer} extends ScanRefer to zero, one or several targets, and labels a description that fits several objects, whether by intent or by vagueness, with all of them. So \emph{the two pillows} and \emph{a pillow}, said of a couch that has two, receive the same label. The two corpora also share sentences and disagree about what they mean: 81\% of Multi3DRefer's multi-target validation sentences paraphrase a ScanRefer sentence about the same scene, labelled with one chair in one corpus and with four in the other. Neither corpus breaks its own rules; the sentence is under-specified and each convention resolves it differently. Our rewrites remove the choice: each says how many objects it refers to, and where each one is. Beacon3D~\cite{beacon3d} finds the same ambiguity by a human study and re-annotates thirty scenes to test on; we measure it geometrically and repair the training corpora at scale. Nanri \etal~\cite{nanri2026viewpoint} note that neither corpus says where the observer stands for \emph{left} and \emph{right}, and give the model the viewpoint as a camera pose; we write the standpoint into the sentence instead, the one input every such model already reads.

\paragraph{Models, and where geometry enters.}
Recent 3D grounding models take in geometry in different ways---object tokens without coordinates~\cite{chatscene}, rotary encodings of object centres~\cite{quatrope}, world coordinates added to visual features~\cite{video3dllm,3drs}, shape features with a distance bias~\cite{3dllava}---yet their ScanRefer scores fall in the same band. Two outside findings point the same way: a language model that never sees the scene scores unexpectedly well on SQA3D~\cite{sqa3d,beacon3d}, and 3D-LLaVA, without fine-tuning, scores near zero on expressions stripped of everything except one direction, such as \emph{the object to the right of the bed}~\cite{nanri2026viewpoint}. We take the common cause to be the text, and test this in \cref{sec:model} by changing the text and leaving the architecture untouched. Training-free methods have begun to repair the text at test time: TDVR~\cite{tdvr} has an LLM disambiguate each query and infer the viewpoint the sentence left out, for its own grounding method. We repair the corpus once, for any model trained on it, and measure the result; where there is no sentence to repair, the same pipeline writes one. Frontier models are usually measured on spatial question answering~\cite{vqa2,vsibench}; we measure them on grounding instead (\cref{sec:model}). Our writer and our reader are given the room as text indexed by object identifiers, the form in which GR3D~\cite{gr3d_zillow} hands a scene's geometry to GPT-5.

\section{What the rewrites contain}
\label{sec:data}

We score 600 single-target expressions from each of the Multi3DRefer and ScanRefer validation sets, 200 per tier of distractor count, and 300 multi-target expressions from Multi3DRefer.
An original and its rewrite pass through the same reader and the same verifier, so every comparison is paired (\cref{tab:data}).

\paragraph{Exactness.}
With no distractor, the score only asks whether the sentence adds one relation that can be checked: 33\% and 40\% of the originals do, on the two corpora.
With distractors, the words must do the work.
At one to four distractors, the originals are certified 15\% and 13\% of the time, at five or more 9\% and 4\%; the rewrites 68\% and 66\%, then 38\% and 28\%.
Crowded rooms remain the hard case for us too.
The gap between the two scores is informative: a rewrite's predicates remove 95\% of the distractors on average, but in a third of the rewrites one predicate, as read and verified, does not hold for the target.
A sentence that says five things has more ways to fail the strict score than a sentence that says two.

\paragraph{Richness.}
A rewrite runs to 39 words against 16 to 18 and, by the reader's count, states 5.2 spatial clauses against about two.
What counts is how many of those the reader can bind to objects and the verifier can check: 3.2 and 3.0 of a rewrite's five, 0.6 and 0.5 of an original's two.
An original says no more when the room is crowded; a rewrite does.
The reason the reader most often gives for an unbound clause in the originals is a missing viewpoint: of their 602 \emph{left} and \emph{right} clauses, 596 do not say from where.
The rewrites use \emph{left} and \emph{right} half as often, 293 clauses, and 277 of them name the standpoint.

\paragraph{Variety and fidelity.}
Over the two validation sets the writer uses 2,438 relation names, all but 171 of them fewer than ten times.
The verifier's 42 predicates cover 87\% of the relations written; the 13\% outside them---attribute words, and free names such as \emph{facing}---do not decide whether a sentence is exact.
That slack is by design: the writer is not held to the verifier's vocabulary, which keeps the writing free and makes the certified rate a floor (\supp{supp:data}).

\paragraph{Several targets.}
Of Multi3DRefer's multi-target descriptions, 89\% contain neither a number nor a plural; 99\% of their rewrites state how many objects are meant.
This is nevertheless our weakest cell: 22\% certified strictly and 39\% leniently, against 7\% for the originals.
A sentence that walks through its members one by one states almost eight clauses, and the reader binds fewer than two.

\paragraph{No original needed.}
Given only a scene and a target with distractors, the pipeline is certified 69\% of the time at one to four distractors and 40\% at five or more on ScanNet, against 68\% and 38\% when it starts from a human sentence.
A new scan corpus costs a label conversion and nothing else: scans from 3RScan and Matterport3D then go through the unchanged pipeline and score in the same range (74\% and 43\% on twelve 3RScan scans; \supp{supp:data}).

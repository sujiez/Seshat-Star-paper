\begin{abstract}
3D vision--language models learn where things are from referring expressions, and for years they have been trained and tested on sentences that, as we show, often do not pin down the object they describe. A referring expression is correct only if it fits its target set exactly: a sentence in which every word is true can still be wrong when it fits one object too many, or one too few. To our knowledge, no published tool scores an arbitrary 3D referring expression on this criterion. We introduce one. A blind reader (a frontier model) translates the sentence into predicates over object ids; a deterministic verifier evaluates them on the point cloud; the sentence is certified exact when the objects satisfying every predicate are precisely the target set. Only about one in five human-written sentences in ScanRefer and Multi3DRefer is certified, and over half contain no checkable spatial relation; human annotators resolve 47\% of a sample. Moreover, 81\% of Multi3DRefer's multi-target sentences are ScanRefer sentences, written about one chair and labelled with four, so the same words carry two labels. Our pipeline, Seshat-Star, rewrites these expressions: a frontier model sees the rendered scene together with the spatial relations a rendering hides, computed by geometry; it decides what to say, and the scorer checks the result. Rewrites are certified nearly three times as often, carry five times as many checkable relations, and are resolved by annotators 70\% of the time. Trained on them, 3D-LLaVA stops hedging between look-alikes: on the \textbf{original} ScanRefer validation set it finds the target as often as before and gains 6 points of Acc@0.5. On targets that only spatial relations tell apart, three grounding models that read a scene as a point cloud, as object tokens and as posed video are each 5 to 24 points more accurate when trained on the rewrites, and a frontier model reading unlabelled renderings resolves 76\% from the rewrite against 58\% from the original. % 11-15: "it gets better at telling look-alikes apart, not at reading longer sentences" cut from the abstract (funnel: that reading lives in intro ¶6 and §6); restore if wanted.

\end{abstract}

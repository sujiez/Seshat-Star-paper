\begin{abstract}
3D vision--language models learn where things are from referring expressions. A referring expression is correct only if it fits its target set exactly: a sentence in which every word is true can still be wrong when it fits one object too many, or one too few. To our knowledge, no published tool scores an arbitrary 3D referring expression on this criterion. We introduce one. A blind reader, a frontier model given an unmarked list of the room's objects, translates the sentence into predicates over object ids; a deterministic verifier evaluates them on the point cloud; the sentence is exact when the objects satisfying every predicate are precisely the target set. By this measure, only about one in five human-written sentences in ScanRefer and Multi3DRefer is exact, and over half contain no checkable spatial relation. Moreover, 81\% of Multi3DRefer's multi-target sentences are ScanRefer sentences, written about one chair and labelled with four, so the same words carry two labels. Our pipeline, Seshat-Star, rewrites these expressions: a frontier model sees the rendered scene together with the relations a rendering hides (viewpoints, rows, stacks, room structure), computed by geometry; it chooses which to use, keeps the annotator's attributes and landmarks, and the scorer checks the result. Rewrites are exact three to four times as often and carry five times as many checkable relations, and the pipeline needs only a scene with instance labels. Trained on the rewrites, 3D-LLaVA gains 6 points of Acc@0.5 on the original ScanRefer validation set, entirely on targets with same-class distractors: it gets better at telling look-alikes apart, not at reading longer sentences. \TODO{Given full geometric relations, a frontier model reaches X\% on the hardest targets, against Y\% from images alone.} We release the rewritten ScanRefer, Multi3DRefer, Nr3D and Sr3D, a distractor-heavy benchmark, and the scorer.
\end{abstract}

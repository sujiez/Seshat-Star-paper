\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Generated referring language for 3D scenes.}
Most 3D referring text that people did not write is produced by one recipe: a scene graph or a program holds the geometry, a template or a constraint sampler turns it into a specification that singles out the target, and a language model paraphrases the result. Sr3D~\cite{referit3d} fills templates over relations that hold for the target and for no other object of its class; SceneVerse~\cite{sceneverse} and 3D-GRAND~\cite{3dgrand} have GPT write from the graph; Disc3D~\cite{disc3d} enumerates subsets of appearance, size and relation modifiers until their intersection is exactly the object; CLEVR-3D-DeRef~\cite{clevr3dderef} executes functional programs~\cite{clevrrefplus} on synthetic scenes; and ViGiL3D++~\cite{vigil3d,vigil3dpp}, the closest work to ours, samples constraints from a graph of real scans---including directions from a sampled standpoint and ordinals along a row---until the solution set is the target. These pipelines differ in detail and agree on three things.
\emph{Uniqueness is guaranteed before the text exists}, on the graph or in the program, and the last step is a free-form rewrite after which nothing returns to the geometry: CLEVR-3D-DeRef scores its paraphrases by how many attribute and relation words they share with the template, not by executing them again, and ViGiL3D++ passes its sentences through a second model to repair grammar and ambiguity.
\emph{The language model is a paraphraser.} What the sentence says is fixed upstream and the model changes the words; Disc3D's vision model is asked whether one rule-given relation holds between two boxed objects in a single frame and, if it says no, chooses a replacement from a closed list; viewpoint words---\emph{front}, \emph{behind}, \emph{left}, \emph{right}---are forbidden outright, and its spatially anchored sentences keep a single distinctive relation. The relation vocabularies are thinnest exactly where human sentences need help: Disc3D's graph records vertical adjacency and horizontal contact and discards every relative direction; ViGiL3D++ does sample a standpoint and emit ordinals, but the standpoint is drawn at random rather than chosen for the reader, and \emph{third from the left} does not require that the objects form a row; what is stacked on what, no pipeline computes. The operators of \cref{sec:pipeline} were built for these cases.
\emph{Nobody measures the finished sentence.} Where the authors did, the cost of the first two choices shows. ViGiL3D++'s own human check finds that single-target sentences whose target has same-class distractors are valid 43.1\% of the time, although each relation in its graph is 89.7\% accurate: composition, missing graph nodes and paraphrase drift are paid for at the sentence and never charged to it. 3D-GRAND reports 91.8\% grounding correctness from crowd-workers asked whether a phrase refers to the boxed object; the same corpus scores 38.3\% when the standard adds \emph{and nothing else fits}~\cite{vigil3dpp}. Disc3D's revision of its test set covered the comparative sentences and left the spatially anchored ones unchecked. Correct but not exact is the normal case in generated text, as we will show it is in human text (\cref{sec:data}).

We differ on all three counts. The model decides what to add to a sentence a person wrote; verification comes \emph{after} the text, on the final sentence; and every sentence receives a score, which is how we can report a number for ScanRefer itself---something no construction-time guarantee can do. Keeping the human sentence also avoids a cost that generating from scratch pays on human benchmarks: a model trained only on ViGiL3D++ scores below the same model trained only on human-written data on ScanRefer and Multi3DRefer (45.5 against 53.4, 23.1 against 33.5), and recovers only when the human data is mixed back in~\cite{vigil3dpp}. Beacon3D~\cite{beacon3d} is complementary rather than competing: it documents, by a human study, that the text of existing grounding benchmarks is ambiguous, and re-annotates thirty scenes to test on; we measure the ambiguity geometrically and repair the training corpora at scale.

\paragraph{The corpora.}
ScanRefer~\cite{scanrefer} attaches one description to one object; Nr3D~\cite{referit3d} does the same through a reference game played among same-class distractors; Multi3DRefer~\cite{multi3drefer} extends ScanRefer to zero, one or several targets, and its convention is that a description matching several objects is labelled with all of them. Under that convention a plural reference (\emph{the two pillows}) and an under-specified one (\emph{a pillow}, with two on the couch) receive the same kind of label. Re-checking the released ScanRefer, Multi3DRefer's authors found 9,324 of its 51,583 descriptions ambiguous~\cite{multi3drefer}. The two corpora thus share words and disagree about what they mean: 81\% of Multi3DRefer's multi-target validation sentences are paraphrases of ScanRefer sentences about the same scene,\footnote{Word Jaccard $\ge 0.5$ with the ScanRefer target among the Multi3DRefer targets; 529 pairs are at $\ge 0.7$ and 39 are near-verbatim.} labelled with one chair in one corpus and with four in the other; conversely, 21\% of the sentences in ScanRefer's \emph{Multiple} validation split were judged by Multi3DRefer's annotators to fit at least two objects, where ScanRefer accepts one. Neither corpus breaks its own rules; the sentence is under-specified and each convention resolves it differently. Nanri \etal~\cite{nanri2026viewpoint} make the related observation that neither corpus handles observer-dependent relations such as \emph{left} and \emph{right}, and build a synthetic corpus in which the viewpoint is supplied to the model as a camera pose; we write the standpoint into the sentence instead, so that no input channel is added (\cref{sec:pipeline}).

\paragraph{Models, and where geometry enters.}
Recent 3D grounding models admit geometry in four different ways. Chat-Scene~\cite{chatscene} represents each object by an identifier token carrying Uni3D and DINOv2 features and no coordinates; QuatRoPE~\cite{quatrope} keeps that recipe and adds object centres to attention through a quaternion rotary encoding; Video-3D-LLM~\cite{video3dllm} and 3DRS~\cite{3drs} add an encoding of each patch's world coordinates to the visual features, the most explicit of the four; 3D-LLaVA~\cite{3dllava} uses sparse U-Net shape features and an isotropic distance bias, with no explicit coordinates and no orientation. The protocols differ (box against mask IoU), so the scores cannot be subtracted, but they sit in one band, 55 to 64 Acc@0.25 on ScanRefer, and adding a coordinate channel to Chat-Scene moved its score by 2.3 points. Third parties have reached the same conclusion from the other side: 3D-LLaVA, evaluated zero-shot on referring expressions that contain nothing but a direction, scores a mean IoU of 0.08~\cite{nanri2026viewpoint}; Beacon3D~\cite{beacon3d} finds that a language model fine-tuned on SQA3D~\cite{sqa3d} without seeing the scene scores unexpectedly well, and that paraphrasing a referring expression moves grounding scores. Training-free methods have begun to repair the text at test time: TDVR~\cite{tdvr} has an LLM disambiguate each query against a scene graph and infer the viewpoint the sentence left out, inside one grounding method, and reports the best zero-shot accuracy on ScanRefer so far. We repair the corpus once, for every omission the scorer can name and for any model trained on it, and measure that the repair holds. Our reading is not that these models cannot use geometry but that the text they are scored on seldom requires it. \cref{sec:model} asks what happens when it does, with the architecture of 3D-LLaVA untouched\TODO{; further architectures pending}. Frontier models are usually measured on spatial question answering~\cite{vqa2,beacon3d}: GR3D~\cite{gr3d_zillow} raises GPT-5 on VSI-Bench~\cite{vsibench} by handing it the boxes and primitives of a scene reconstructed from a few frames, as text indexed by object identifiers, and finds that the gain disappears when the identifiers are not also marked in the image---the identifier-bound design our writer and reader rely on (\cref{sec:pipeline,sec:scorer}). We measure them on grounding instead, with the full scan and its instance masks (\cref{sec:model}).
